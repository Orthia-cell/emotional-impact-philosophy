# Phase 1: Initial Exploration

## Date: March 18, 2026

### Core Themes Emerging

#### 1. Affective Aesthetics & Experimental Philosophy
- **Mario Attie-Picker et al. (2024)**: People choose sad music not for the emotions they *feel*, but for the emotions they *perceive* in the music. Emotional expressiveness > technical proficiency in judgments about music's value.
- **Key tension**: The paradox isn't about felt emotions, but perceived emotions in the art itself.
- **Cross-cultural question**: Recognition of emotions in music depends on familiarity with local musical grammar (Bonard 2019) — yet some elements (aversion to dissonance) appear universal.

#### 2. Intersubjectivity: The Space Between
- **Husserl/Merleau-Ponty**: Intersubjective intentionality — consciousness is inherently relational
- **Key distinction**: Intersubjectivity ≠ consensus. It's shared understanding, mutual awareness, the *capacity* for empathy without requiring agreement
- **Three components**: Shared emotion/affect (attunement), shared attention, shared intention

#### 3. Collective Emotions (Thonhauser)
- **Crucial insight**: Collective emotions are not emotions *about* collectives, but emotions *of* collectives
- **Dynamical self-organization**: The collective emotion emerges from integration of individual contributions
- **First-person plural awareness**: "We feel" — distinct from individual feelings, yet not a Borg-like fusion
- **Reflection test**: Individual reflection can *confirm* the collective nature of experience

#### 4. Arendt's Plurality: The Anti-Consensus
- **"Men, not Man, live on the earth"**: Plurality as the human condition
- **Representative thinking**: "Enlarged mentality" — considering perspectives of those absent
- **Judgment without truth**: Political judgments are doxai (opinions), not truth claims
- **Anti-despotic**: Truth eliminates debate; plurality *preserves* debate
- **Sensus communis**: Not common sense as consensus, but sense that "takes account of the mode of representation of all other men in thought"

#### 5. Epistemic Sentimentalism
- **Modest version**: Emotional experiences provide *prima facie* justification for evaluative beliefs
- **Awe experiences**: Provide justification for believing something is sublime
- **Connection to project**: Emotional impact as a form of epistemic access

### Initial Questions

1. **If consensus is impossible, what gives emotional impact its "gravity"?**
   - Massumi's "affect" vs "emotion" distinction — affect is intensity, virtual, contains potential
   - Perhaps emotional impact operates at the level of *affect* (pre-cognitive, pre-consensual)

2. **What is the relationship between individual emotional response and collective meaning?**
   - Arendt: Judgment is always "in an anticipated communication with others"
   - Yet: "The more people's standpoints I have present... the more valid my final conclusions"
   - Validity without consensus?

3. **Can we have "wrong" emotional responses?**
   - Seidel & Prinz: Music can induce emotions that modify moral evaluations
   - But: Cross-cultural variation in emotional recognition suggests emotional responses are culturally mediated

4. **What does it mean for something to "land" emotionally without being agreed upon?**
   - Thonhauser's "emotional fusion" vs "emotional segregation"
   - The concert example: Friends experiencing collective joy while maintaining individual awareness

### Tensions to Explore

| Tension | Side A | Side B |
|---------|--------|--------|
| Individual vs Collective | Private emotional experience | Shared/intersubjective meaning |
| Consensus vs Validity | Agreement as goal | Arendt: plurality preserves debate |
| Affect vs Emotion | Massumi: pre-cognitive intensity | Cognitive, qualified, conventional |
| Universality vs Particularity | Cross-cultural recognition | Cultural grammar dependence |
| Landing vs Agreement | "It moved me" | "We agree it moved us" |
